Levels, not dates. **0** = Make Repo ours · **1** = fix what exists · **2** = minimum for real daily use · **3** = near-term (each ≈ days to 2 weeks of mixed daily work) · **4** = north star (homestead → small-town scale, 2–4 decade horizon). New ideas land in the Inbox until a sorting pass — never straight into a level.

Settled on area (`900 ÷ 75 = 12`) and floor, on two grounds: 36 implies 25 cm² per radish against square-foot gardening's 58 cm² (16 per square foot), i.e. 2.3× denser than a famously intensive method; and over-sowing is the asymmetric mistake for a layperson — crowded radishes never bulb and a sowing can't be undone, while under-sowing merely costs yield. The runner-up was equidistant at √(5×15) = 8.66 cm → 3×3 = **9**, rejected because floor-rounding discards up to 44% of a cell and jumps unevenly as cell size changes, which matters once cell size becomes per-bed (next item). The fix is per-plant row spacing reviewed for bed growing (carrot, onion at `10 × 30`, bean at `10 × 40`), not a denser formula for all 45.*

New `src/lib/bedGeometry.ts` (`bedCellSizeCm`, `bedCellAreaM2`, `bedSizeM`, `bedAreaM2`, `plantAreaM2`) accumulates per bed instead, with 6 tests including one asserting the wrong answer is **not** produced (4 cells at 30 cm + 2 at 50 cm = 0.86 m², not 6 × 0.09). `calculateSufficiency` aggregates separately from `estimatePlantArea`, so it got its own test rather than being assumed covered.*

The first cut of this had the invariant backwards — it held the grid and let the footprint move, which made cell size a bed-resizer. It is not a hardware problem and debouncing is the wrong primitive: it delays the hang and still commits a pathological value if you stop typing at "1". `CellSizeInput` holds its own text and commits a valid number on blur/Enter only, so intermediate values never reach the store. It **also commits on unmount**, because "press Enter to apply" is not discoverable and closing the panel plainly reads as applying — done at unmount rather than on the gear specifically, so bed collapse and view-mode switches are covered by the same guarantee and blur/unmount cannot race.   - ***A hard cell cap now exists, because this change made one necessary.*** *Raising the bed max from 20 m to 200 m (needed for field beds) put a 200 × 200 m bed at 30 cm — 444,889 cells — one form away. `MAX_BED_CELLS = 10_000` permits everything sane (a 40 × 20 m field at 30 cm is 8,911) and refuses the rest rather than silently clamping to a grid nobody asked for. Enforced in both paths: `regridToCellSize` returns null, and the New Bed modal disables Add with a reason. Verified live — 40 × 20 m at 5 cm (800 × 400 = 320,000) is blocked and does not hang, since the modal only renders text.*
  - ***Post-creation editing is allowed only while the bed is empty.*** *Re-gridding cannot preserve plantings — a 30 cm cell is not a 15 cm cell — so rather than a confirm dialog that people click through, the input disables with "Clear the bed to change cell size". The existing "Clear all" button makes the destructive step the gardener's own action. This still beats the alternative of no post-creation control at all, where fixing a wrong choice means delete-and-recreate, losing notes, paths, environment config and raised-bed height too. Path keys are grid coordinates, so a re-grid clears `paths`.*
  - ***The global setting stays, as the default it now is*** *— relabelled "Default cell size" in all four locales. The store field keeps the name `gridCellSizeCm` on purpose: it is persisted in `gardener-storage`, in `dataExport`/`dataImport`, and in `useBackendSync`, so renaming it is a migration, not a rename. Inside the four **bed-scoped** libs (`placementValidation`, `bedRecommendation`, `plantDensity`, `soilVolume`) the parameter **is** renamed to `cellSizeCm`, because a bed-scoped function taking something called "grid" invites exactly the misreading this item exists to remove.*
  - ***Both lossy boundaries closed, same bug class as the earlier planner import:*** *`gardenImport` rebuilds beds field by field, so it now carries `cellSizeCm` (and rejects `0`/non-numeric, since absent must keep meaning "inherit" rather than "zero-area bed"); `sharing` gained `cs` on the template, because a 6 × 4 bed at 25 cm is not the same layout as one at 30 cm. A link without `cs` still decodes — the recipient's default applies — so old share URLs keep working.*
  - ***The two density boxes had their visual weight inverted, and it was swapped.*** *`PlantInfoPanel`'s prominent bordered callout showed the **default** cell size (the panel is not bed-scoped — a plant is picked before a bed is), while the plain line in `CellEditor` showed the **actual** bed's figure. The eye lands on the pretty one, so the pretty one has to be the true one: `PlantInfoPanel` drops to a plain chip beside sun/water/spacing ("12 per 30 cm", with a title attribute pointing at the caveat), and `CellEditor` gets the bordered callout, now naming the bed's own size ("Sow about 12 plants in this 30 cm square"). Generic data no longer outranks true data.*
  - ***Footprint is preserved only to the nearest whole cell,*** *which the copy now admits ("the bed keeps its size, give or take a part cell"). A 1.8 m bed re-gridded to 1 m cells becomes 2.0 m, because 1.8 does not divide by 1 and a grid cannot hold a part cell. ## Level 2 — Minimum for daily real use (mid-season, real garden)

- [x] **Mid-season onboarding = tasks become computed, never stored.** — *`src/lib/taskGeneration.ts` (pure, 15 tests) is now the whole task list: `generateTasks(gardens, plantMap, {today, lastFrostDate, horizonDays})`. `taskSlice` keeps the gardener's own tasks plus a `taskOverlay` of verdicts; `useTasks()` merges them into one `TaskItem[]` in a memo. The Generate button is gone.*
  - ***The key is the planting GROUP, not the cell, and that is a deliberate departure from the wording above.*** *Keying per `CellPlanting.id` produces twelve identical "Sow Tomato" rows for a twelve-cell block — unusable. A job is (bedId, plantId, plantedDate), which is stable when a plant moves within its bed, survives adding a thirteenth cell, and is exactly the shape the L3 planting read-model ("plantId, area, count, plantedDate, locationRef") will formalize. Cell ids still earn their keep on their own item's grounds — merge correctness and the L3 aggregation — but honesty demands recording that item 1 did **not** turn out to need them. The row shows "3 squares" so the count isn't lost.*
  - ***The split is planned vs in-the-ground, and that is the whole mid-season fix.*** *A cell with `plantedDate` never emits a sowing task and gets a harvest date from when it actually went in; a cell without one gets sowing/transplant tasks and no harvest. `plantedDate` had been written by `CellEditor.tsx:74` and read by literally nothing.*
  - ***Frost offsets survive in exactly one function***, *`prePlantingTasks`, with a header naming it as the debt item 4 and the L3 engine replace. Deleting it now would leave a pre-season gardener with no answer to "when do I sow this"; fencing it means the engine swaps into one place. Nothing else in the file reads an offset.*
  - ***Expiry is per-rule, which is what "past tasks simply aren't generated" means in practice.*** *A sowing window gets 21 days of grace then stops being generated — that is the actual fix for the March-dates-in-July bug. A harvest task runs to `plantedDate + harvestDaysMax + 14`, because the crop is still in the ground and a missed sowing is not. Forward horizon is 120 days.*
  - ***Perennials are skipped rather than guessed.*** *5 of 45 plants carry `harvestDaysMax >= 365` (raspberry, blueberry, currant, gooseberry, asparagus) — the placeholder the lifecycle split exists to kill. "Harvest your blueberry 730 days after planting" is worse than silence, so they emit nothing until `BearingMaturity` lands with item 4.*
  - ***Three things fell out for free.*** *Titles are built at render from `{plant, bed}`, so the list follows the UI language — the old generator translated once and **stored** the string, freezing a list in whatever language made it. iCal `UID` is now the stable key instead of `task-${Date.now()++}`, so re-exporting updates calendar events instead of duplicating them. And "Clear completed" is gone, correctly: an expired task stops being generated, so there is nothing to sweep, and the button would only have resurrected tasks.*
  - ***Migration v5*** *drops stored tasks carrying a `plantId` (only the generator ever set one) and keeps the rest; `dataImport` applies the same rule to older backups, so a v1 export can't resurrect a frozen list. Verdicts are retired at startup against live **group** keys, not task keys — matching on task keys would discard a verdict merely for sitting beyond today's horizon.*
  - ***The first cut had harvest require `plantedDate`, and that was wrong — caught by using it.*** *`plantedDate` is optional and nothing sets it, so the ordinary case (draw a plan, never type a date) produced no harvest task ever, while the sowing tasks expired and left nothing behind. Meanwhile `SeasonTimeline` derives its harvest bar from the frost offsets and kept showing one, so the two surfaces disagreed and the task list was the one that was wrong. Harvest now applies to **every** planting: a recorded `plantedDate` wins, and without one the date comes from `impliedPlantingDate`, which the timeline now imports instead of keeping its own copy — the duplication is what let them drift. Estimated dates are labelled `estimated` in the list, so a worked-out date is never dressed up as a recorded one. Lesson worth keeping: the rule looked right in isolation and had passing tests; what exposed it was filling a bed the way a user would.*
  - ***Ticking is reversible and visibly so.*** *Un-ticking deletes the verdict rather than storing a negative, so the task is simply computed again. Ticked and dismissed rows stay on screen struck through until you leave the page — a mis-click gets undone where it happened rather than behind the Completed filter — while the stat counts still report the truth. `completeTask` was one-way (inherited from upstream) and became `setTaskDone(id, done)`, since after computed tasks gained un-ticking, a typed-in task was the only thing that could not be un-ticked.*
  - *Verified in the running app: a tomato planted 2026-06-01 gave one harvest task on 2026-07-31 (= `plantedDate + harvestDaysMin`) reading "3 squares" across its three cells, with no sowing tasks; ticking survived a reload; German rendered "Tomate - Bed A / 3 Felder / Ernten"; clearing the bed retired the verdict on the next page load. And on an auto-filled bed with no dates anywhere: Thyme 14.07 and Corn 07.08 in the list match the timeline's tooltips exactly, while Dill (window closed 30.06) and Garlic (09.02) are correctly absent.*

### The climate block (items 1–4 below, in order)

*Restructured 2026-07-28 after asking whether incremental delivery was itself
the drag. It mostly is not — the Level 1 haul (lossy import, undo writing
during render, `monthFraction` dividing every month by 31, auto-fill planting
through paths, the two-bed collapse bug) was **inherited** and found by using
the app, not created by building it in steps. Three defects were genuinely
self-inflicted across ~15 items, two caught in review.*

*What **is** a drag is deferring the schema break. Frost offsets are declared
dead in CLAUDE.md yet live in 10 non-test files, so every new feature has to
decide how to relate to a dead abstraction — `taskGeneration.prePlantingTasks`
is fenced off for exactly that reason. And the offsets **cannot be deleted
without the engine that replaces them**, which is why "schema v2" alone was
never a coherent item: it would leave both models live at once. So the schema,
its input, and the solver move as one block, and the ERA5 fetch stays at Level
3 because manual ClimateProfile entry already covers the input.*

- [x] **Stop protecting local data — do this first, it makes the rest cheaper.** Decided 2026-07-28: the app has no users, and its one tester does not use it for real (and when that changes it will be a self-hosted Docker instance on TrueNAS, not this dev environment or the Pages demo). Both "fixes" npm offers are downgrades. Revisit the react-router 8 migration on its own merits rather than as a security scramble.
- Auto-fill caps species variety at a fixed 6 (4 for `calories`/`yield`) however large the bed: `Math.min(6, Math.ceil(totalCells / 3))` in `recommendBedPlanting`. The low end does scale — a 6-cell bed gets 2 species — but the top end never moves, so a 96-cell bed and a 400-cell bed both get 6. Not obviously a bug: fewer types is the deliberate point of `calories`/`yield`, and monoculture blocks are legitimate at field scale. Wants sorting alongside the Level 3 rescoring and the fields abstraction rather than a blind raise.
- *(sorted from the 2026-07-25 idea batch — parked deliberately, each with its unlock condition)*
- **Sensor integration** (pH, moisture, fertility probes): no single open standard exists — the closest ecosystem is MQTT/Home Assistant. If it ever happens, the right shape is *accepting imported readings*, never device drivers. Fails the layperson test today; Level 4+ at best.
- **Soil pH gating recommendations:** coherent with the environment-check pattern (optional bed field, ignorable by default, `limitedBy`-style reason), but needs authored pH ranges per plant *and* users who know their pH. Wait for a large verified corpus. Public soil data (SoilGrids, CC-BY) could someday prefill it from location — depends on this existing first.
- **Mid-season diagnostics** ("yellowing leaves might mean…", pruning tips for poor producers): an authored plant-care encyclopedia by another name — large content cost, drifts the app toward reference book. The Level 4 local-AI Q&A line is the cheaper route to the same value; re-evaluate against it, not on its own.
- **Culling recommendations:** not a feature — an *output* of things already planned. End of computed window + rotation memory + backwards planning ≈ "this window closed, the next crop wants in." Don't build a separate culling engine.
- **Forever bed** (continuously replanted day-by-day, never reset between seasons): revisit once succession presets and backwards planning both exist — it may fall out of them nearly free. Standalone, it's a combinatorial money pit.
- **Rice paddy:** a new (flooded) environment type plus water infrastructure; only sensible after Level 4 water planning. Kept so it isn't lost — it's fun.
- **Wood pile / firewood ops** and **fiber/fabric plants** (flax, rope crops): non-food self-sufficiency. Real homestead life, but behind the food thesis — belongs to a hypothetical future "homestead ops" wing, not to any current level. Fiber is cheap if it's ever just more plants plus a use tag.
- **Vertical growing** (herb tubes, towers): a new container geometry the environment-type system could absorb someday. No pull for it yet.
- **Soil mixture / amendment recommendations from prior plantings:** the adjacent real feature is Level 3 rotation memory (season archives already hold the history). Amendment advice needs authored per-plant data; consider as a rotation-memory extension, not standalone.
- **OpenPlantbook import:** its data is sensor-threshold-oriented (min/max moisture/light for the Home Assistant crowd) and community-grade; ClimateNeeds semantics are richer than what it offers, and its licensing is unverified against the no-NC/SA rule. Skip unless the sensor idea revives.
- **localStorage → IndexedDB migration:** localStorage caps at ~5MB and rewrites the whole state as one JSON string per save; IndexedDB is the browser's real local database (async, effectively unbounded for our sizes) and is *exactly as offline* — same device, same browser, no server involved. Contained change: Zustand `persist` takes a pluggable storage backend; first launch copies `gardener-storage` over, users notice nothing. When it lands, also call `navigator.storage.persist()` so the browser won't evict data under disk pressure. Unlock condition: years of season archives + a 300-plant corpus pressing the ceiling — not before.
- **Native-only / native-wildlife auto-fill filters:** unlike low-water (data already exists), native-ness is a whole new authored data axis — range maps per plant, fuzzy by region. Park until there's a data story; don't let it ride the cheap-filter wave.
