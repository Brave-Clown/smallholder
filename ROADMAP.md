# Roadmap

Levels, not dates. **0** = Make Repo ours · **1** = fix what exists · **2** = minimum for real daily use · **3** = near-term (each ≈ days to 2 weeks of mixed daily work) · **4** = north star (homestead → small-town scale, 2–4 decade horizon). New ideas land in the Inbox until a sorting pass — never straight into a level.

- [x] Show plant spacing prominently at placement time — *new `src/lib/plantDensity.ts` returns `{ perCell, cellsPerPlant, areaPerPlantCm2 }` from `spacingCm × rowSpacingCm` against `gridCellSizeCm²`, with `src/test/plantDensity.test.ts` (6 cases, including one that asserts a usable figure for all 45 shipped plants). Plants larger than a cell invert to "One plant needs about 4 squares of 30 cm" (tomato 50 × 70), which the naive formula would have rendered as `0`. Verified in the running app at both surfaces; keys added to all four locales.*
  - *The **12 was challenged and re-derived**, since it started as an offhand number. Three candidate readings: squaring `spacingCm` → 36, interior 5 cm intersections → 25, both fields → 12. The intersection reading is geometrically wrong here — cells tile, so its edge margin would leave 10 cm dead gaps at every cell boundary; the real fork was 36 vs 12. Settled on area (`900 ÷ 75 = 12`) and floor, on two grounds: 36 implies 25 cm² per radish against square-foot gardening's 58 cm² (16 per square foot), i.e. 2.3× denser than a famously intensive method; and over-sowing is the asymmetric mistake for a layperson — crowded radishes never bulb and a sowing can't be undone, while under-sowing merely costs yield. The runner-up was equidistant at √(5×15) = 8.66 cm → 3×3 = **9**, rejected because floor-rounding discards up to 44% of a cell and jumps unevenly as cell size changes, which matters once cell size becomes per-bed (next item). Display-only, as scoped: sufficiency math stays area-based and `CellPlanting` is still one `plantId` per cell, so the count is a sowing instruction, not a model change.*
  - *Left alone deliberately: enforcement. The earlier parenthetical claiming manual placement ignores spacing was already corrected — `validatePlacement` warns `validation.tooClose` under 40% of `spacingCm` (`placementValidation.ts:79`), which at a 30 cm grid can never fire for radish (40% of 5 cm = 2 cm). That threshold is about same-species cell adjacency and is a separate question from per-cell density; changing it was out of scope here.*
  - *Data-quality snag worth its own pass, not fixed here: `rowSpacingCm` is sometimes a field-agriculture hoeing-access figure rather than a bed spacing. Carrot is `5 × 30` → **6 per cell** against square-foot gardening's 16, so the formula reads as over-conservative for exactly the crops grown in dense bands. The fix is per-plant row spacing reviewed for bed growing (carrot, onion at `10 × 30`, bean at `10 × 40`), not a denser formula for all 45.*

  - ***Soil volume:*** *new `src/lib/soilVolume.ts` (`raisedBedSoilVolume`, 4 tests) turns the previously write-only `heightCm` into "Filling this bed takes about 1728 L of soil — 1.73 m³, or 44 bags of 40 L". The whole footprint counts, paths included: a path drawn on a raised bed is a walkway over the same box of soil, not a hole in it. Shown in `RaisedBedConfigPanel`, which now appears in **both** the bed config panel and the New Bed modal, so the cost of an 80 cm default is visible before the bed exists. That exposed a third copy of the metres→cells rounding (`handleCreateBed`, the `gridInfo` line, and now the volume preview each rounded independently); all three go through one `cellsForMetres` helper, so the preview cannot disagree with the bed that gets created.*

Frost weeks are pluralised through i18next `count` (it read "1 weeks" first time round).*
  - ***Four decorative fields deliberately left, not deleted*** *— removing them would destroy data users have already entered, and each has a real wiring waiting rather than being fluff: `greenhouseConfig.material` and `ventilation` (glass vs plastic, automatic venting → should soften the `maxTempC` heat alert in `weatherAlerts.ts:51`), `greenhouseConfig.heatingType` (belongs with Level 3 costs/ROI), and `containerConfig.material` (terracotta and fabric dry far faster than plastic → watering guidance). `RaisedBedConfig.layers` was stranger — typed with an i18n label in all four locales, but no input wrote it and nothing read it — and it has now been **deleted**, type and labels. Checked against the whole roadmap first: nothing in Levels 1–4 or the inbox needs bed layering, and the two soil-adjacent inbox items (pH gating, amendment recommendations) are already served by `SoilTest` and `Amendment` in `types/soil.ts` — both per-`bedId`, dated and quantified, with `AmendmentType` already covering compost and mulch. Decisive point: if hügelkultur layering is ever built it would need material + depth + date and would apply to any deep bed, not a bare `string[]` on the raised-bed config, so this field reserved nothing for it. No user data was at risk since nothing ever wrote it; a legacy import carrying the key keeps it as an inert extra property, which is not worth code to strip.*
- [x] Grid cell size becomes a per-bed property instead of a global setting — *`Bed.cellSizeCm?: number`, where **undefined means inherit the app default**. That optionality is the whole trick: no migration, no touched localStorage, and every existing garden keeps its geometry — which matters because the storage-key rename is deliberately reserved for the Level 2 schema-v2 migration.*
  - ***The substance was not the field, it was three aggregation sites that silently assumed uniform cells.*** *`estimatePlantArea`, the inline accumulator inside `calculateSufficiency`, and `FoodPlan`'s `cropPlans` all counted cells across every bed and multiplied once by a single cell size — correct only while all beds agree. New `src/lib/bedGeometry.ts` (`bedCellSizeCm`, `bedCellAreaM2`, `bedSizeM`, `bedAreaM2`, `plantAreaM2`) accumulates per bed instead, with 6 tests including one asserting the wrong answer is **not** produced (4 cells at 30 cm + 2 at 50 cm = 0.86 m², not 6 × 0.09). `calculateSufficiency` aggregates separately from `estimatePlantArea`, so it got its own test rather than being assumed covered.*
  - ***Metres are the real-world fact; cells are only planning resolution.*** *So changing cell size **preserves the footprint and re-grids the bed** (`regridToCellSize`, 4 tests): a 1.8 × 1.2 m bed goes 6 × 4 at 30 cm → 3 × 2 at 60 cm and stays 1.8 × 1.2 m. The first cut of this had the invariant backwards — it held the grid and let the footprint move, which made cell size a bed-resizer. That is nonsense against the physical model (you do not rebuild a bed because you changed your planning grid) and it was caught in review, not by the tests, because both behaviours are self-consistent. Worth remembering: a green suite does not tell you the invariant is the right one.*
  - ***The control that actually matters is at creation***, *and the first cut missed it entirely — the New Bed modal computed cells from the global default with no way to override. It now sits beside width/height, and it is the whole point of the feature: a 40 × 20 m field at the 30 cm default is **133 × 67 = 8,911 cells**; at 1 m cells it is 40 × 20 = 800. (Level 3's fields-as-rows item still stands — this makes big beds usable, not efficient.)*
  - ***The input was a self-inflicted footgun, caught in review.*** *Committing per keystroke meant backspacing "30" clamped to the minimum instead of clearing the field, and typing "10" re-gridded at 1 cm on the way through — 180 × 120 = **21,600 rendered cells** on a small bed, which hangs the page. It is not a hardware problem and debouncing is the wrong primitive: it delays the hang and still commits a pathological value if you stop typing at "1". `CellSizeInput` holds its own text and commits a valid number on blur/Enter only, so intermediate values never reach the store. It **also commits on unmount**, because "press Enter to apply" is not discoverable and closing the panel plainly reads as applying — done at unmount rather than on the gear specifically, so bed collapse and view-mode switches are covered by the same guarantee and blur/unmount cannot race. Verified live: typing 1 → 10 → 100 left the stored grid untouched, and closing the panel with a pending 60 committed it (6 × 4 → 3 × 2) without Enter.*
  - ***A hard cell cap now exists, because this change made one necessary.*** *Raising the bed max from 20 m to 200 m (needed for field beds) put a 200 × 200 m bed at 30 cm — 444,889 cells — one form away. `MAX_BED_CELLS = 10_000` permits everything sane (a 40 × 20 m field at 30 cm is 8,911) and refuses the rest rather than silently clamping to a grid nobody asked for. Enforced in both paths: `regridToCellSize` returns null, and the New Bed modal disables Add with a reason. Verified live — 40 × 20 m at 5 cm (800 × 400 = 320,000) is blocked and does not hang, since the modal only renders text.*
  - ***Post-creation editing is allowed only while the bed is empty.*** *Re-gridding cannot preserve plantings — a 30 cm cell is not a 15 cm cell — so rather than a confirm dialog that people click through, the input disables with "Clear the bed to change cell size". The existing "Clear all" button makes the destructive step the gardener's own action. This still beats the alternative of no post-creation control at all, where fixing a wrong choice means delete-and-recreate, losing notes, paths, environment config and raised-bed height too. Path keys are grid coordinates, so a re-grid clears `paths`.*
  - ***The global setting stays, as the default it now is*** *— relabelled "Default cell size" in all four locales. The store field keeps the name `gridCellSizeCm` on purpose: it is persisted in `gardener-storage`, in `dataExport`/`dataImport`, and in `useBackendSync`, so renaming it is a migration, not a rename. Inside the four **bed-scoped** libs (`placementValidation`, `bedRecommendation`, `plantDensity`, `soilVolume`) the parameter **is** renamed to `cellSizeCm`, because a bed-scoped function taking something called "grid" invites exactly the misreading this item exists to remove.*
  - ***Both lossy boundaries closed, same bug class as the earlier planner import:*** *`gardenImport` rebuilds beds field by field, so it now carries `cellSizeCm` (and rejects `0`/non-numeric, since absent must keep meaning "inherit" rather than "zero-area bed"); `sharing` gained `cs` on the template, because a 6 × 4 bed at 25 cm is not the same layout as one at 30 cm. A link without `cs` still decodes — the recipient's default applies — so old share URLs keep working.*
  - ***The two density boxes had their visual weight inverted, and it was swapped.*** *`PlantInfoPanel`'s prominent bordered callout showed the **default** cell size (the panel is not bed-scoped — a plant is picked before a bed is), while the plain line in `CellEditor` showed the **actual** bed's figure. The eye lands on the pretty one, so the pretty one has to be the true one: `PlantInfoPanel` drops to a plain chip beside sun/water/spacing ("12 per 30 cm", with a title attribute pointing at the caveat), and `CellEditor` gets the bordered callout, now naming the bed's own size ("Sow about 12 plants in this 30 cm square"). Generic data no longer outranks true data.*
  - ***Footprint is preserved only to the nearest whole cell,*** *which the copy now admits ("the bed keeps its size, give or take a part cell"). A 1.8 m bed re-gridded to 1 m cells becomes 2.0 m, because 1.8 does not divide by 1 and a grid cannot hold a part cell. Rounding to nearest keeps the error smallest; the panel shows the resulting metres, so the truth is always on screen.*
  - ***Known limitation, deliberate:*** *`PlantInfoPanel` still speaks for the default size rather than any bed — unavoidable while it renders before a bed is chosen, and mitigated by the string naming the size and by `CellEditor` carrying the bed-specific number. Verified live: creating a 40 × 20 m bed at 100 cm stored `{width: 40, height: 20, cellSizeCm: 100}`; re-gridding an empty 6 × 4 bed to 60 cm gave 3 × 2 while the footprint stayed 1.80 × 1.20 m; the planted bed's input was disabled with the clear-the-bed note while the empty ones stayed editable; and resetting to the default removed the key entirely rather than writing 30 back, so the bed genuinely inherits.*

## Level 2 — Minimum for daily real use (mid-season, real garden)

- [ ] **Mid-season onboarding = tasks become computed, never stored.** The task list for any date is a pure function of (plantings × rules × today), recomputed on render; the store keeps only a thin user overlay — done/dismissed/snoozed/manual — keyed by stable identity (plantingId + taskType + cycleIndex). Past tasks aren't "marked implied-done", they simply aren't generated. (Verified root cause of the current bug: tasks are materialized rows in `taskSlice`, dated off `lastFrostDate`; `plantedDate` is ignored.) This is the same invariant as planting windows applied to tasks, and it's load-bearing downstream: maintenance tasks (L3) become more rules in the function, worker sheets (L4) a partition of its output, time scrubbing (L4) the same function called with a different date. Most real users adopt tools mid-season; this is the adoption showstopper.
- [ ] Dual frost dates (spring + fall) with risk percentiles, frost-free toggle, manual ClimateProfile entry
- [ ] Per-bed sun exposure field, wired into placement validation and auto-fill scoring
- [ ] Schema v2 migration: lifecycle split, required ClimateNeeds, family on the plant, frost offsets deleted. Add `rootDepthCm` while the migration is open — schema fields are cheapest now, and batch-1 plants should be authored with it (the depth *check* itself is Level 3). Also while it's open: **every entity gets a UUID + `updatedAt`** (gardens, beds, plantings, overlay entries). Costs nothing now, brutal to retrofit; it's the prerequisite for export/import merging (L3), multi-site rollups (L4), and any future sync — we don't build sync, we just refuse to make it impossible.
- [ ] Plant display names move from locale files into the plant data (with optional per-locale overrides); locale files keep UI strings only. At 45 plants the current split is fine; at 300 community-contributed plants, "touch five files across two trees to add one plant" is the biggest contribution-friction tax. One-file-per-plant with a build step can follow when the batch pipeline lands (L3) — it makes "add a plant" a small reviewable PR, and draft → verified maps exactly onto PR review.
- [ ] Bed size presets at creation (4×4 ft, 6×4 ft, a few common container sizes) — stored in cm, imperial labels only at the display boundary
- [ ] **Sanity-check each auto-fill strategy against its own goal.** Nothing currently asserts that `selfsufficient` actually produces better nutritional coverage than `balanced`, or that `calories` beats the others on calories — the strategies are named promises with no measurement behind them. `calculateSufficiency` already returns per-nutrient `{produced, needed, percent}`, so it is the oracle: fill a bed with each strategy, score the result, assert each one wins on the metric it claims. Land this *before* crop batch 1, so the batch is measured against a known baseline instead of silently moving one.
- [ ] Crop expansion batch 1 (~50 homestead/SoCal staples: sweet potato, okra, melons, winged bean, first fruit trees)
- [ ] Task list filterable by garden/bed selection
- [ ] README status rewrite: once mid-season onboarding works, drop the "use upstream instead" steer and the early-fork badge, and re-describe what actually ships

## Level 3 — Near-term

- [ ] Window engine: ERA5 fetch → weekly normals + frost percentiles → computed PlantingWindows with risk levels and `limitedBy` reasons. (ERA5 is global — EU and China coverage come for free; Open-Meteo exposes it as a free API. International support is the existing design, not a future feature.)
- [ ] **Backwards planning:** plan from harvest date instead of sow date — "what still harvests before winter here", "something to pick every few weeks". Serves both the beginner and the survivor persona. This is the window engine run in reverse: once windows carry maturity dates it's a filter, not a new engine, so sequence it right after.
- [ ] Rotation memory: read season archives, warn on same-family repeats per bed across years
- [ ] Sites above gardens (site → garden → bed)
- [ ] **Fields as a row-based abstraction** — crop, row length, row count, row spacing — not giant cell grids (a 100 m × 100 m bed at 30 cm cells ≈ 111,000 DOM nodes; rows are also how field-scale agriculture actually thinks)
- [ ] **Plantings as the shared read-model** (land together with fields): a normalized shape — plantId, area, count, plantedDate, locationRef — that cells, rows, and containers each know how to produce, and that sufficiency, tasks, rotation memory, and the window engine consume *exclusively*. Today the intelligence reads geometry directly (`estimatePlantArea` loops `bed.cells`); with three geometries that becomes per-geometry branches smeared across every lib. Geometry becomes an input format, intelligence goes geometry-blind — and town-scale aggregation reduces to summing plantings across sites.
- [ ] Auto harvest + pick-window tasks (beans: "pick every 2–3 days" once bearing)
- [ ] **Maintenance task generation** (weeding, pest/rot checks, drip-line checks, animal-sign walks) — extends the same generator as harvest tasks. Every category opt-in, or the task list becomes a nag machine and violates ignorability. Solo gardener only; assigning to workers stays Level 4.
- [ ] **Multi-cell plant footprints:** a pumpkin placed in one 30 cm cell should visibly claim its real spread into neighbouring cells, and validation should know about the overlap. Auto-fill already respects spacing; manual placement has no visual truth. Rendering + grid-model work — meatier than it sounds, not a quick win.
- [ ] Root-depth check: `rootDepthCm` (added in the Level 2 schema) vs container volume and raised-bed height, joining the existing environment-fit checks — today the auto tool will happily put a 30 cm root in a 15 cm bed
- [ ] Nursery/propagation bed purpose + propagation methods (seed / cutting / division / sets / tuber / crown)
- [ ] Imperial display units (storage stays metric, conversion at the boundary)
- [ ] Granular export/import and sharing at bed and site level (notes included)
- [ ] Chinese (zh) locale
- [ ] **Rescore auto-fill for a growing plant corpus** (follows the Level 2 sanity-check). Two structural problems, both harmless at 45 plants and neither harmless at 300: (1) `scorePlant` grades every plant independently and takes the top N, but "self-sufficient" is a property of the *set* — six high-protein legumes each score well and leave the plan with no vitamin C. A coverage goal needs portfolio selection, not per-plant ranking. (2) The nutrition thresholds are absolute (`protein > 2`, `calories > 30`, `vitC > 20`) and today split the corpus 16/25/22 of 45; nothing rescales them as plants land, so the signal flattens toward "everything qualifies". Also `?? 0` scores missing nutrition as zero, so sparse `draft` entries rank bottom for reasons unrelated to their merit. Add a **low-water goal/filter** here while the strategies are being rethought — `waterNeed` already exists on every plant, so it's nearly free; a pollinator/bee-attractor filter can ride along if the data field lands with the batches.
- [ ] Crop batches 2+ toward hundreds (draft → verified review pipeline). Batch themes, data not features: perennial long-term feeders (berry bushes — lifecycle split exists precisely for this, and "food per unit water" becomes computable once perennial yields and waterNeed coexist); herbs & spices; medicinal plants (traditional-use descriptions only, no health claims, per dataQuality rules); a **WW2 Victory Garden template pack** once its plants exist in the corpus — rides the guild system, doubles as onboarding material
- [ ] Succession presets move from hardcoded tables into plant data

- [ ] Workers: assignable tasks, per-person daily sheets, selection-scoped lists at town scale. Manual assignment plus even split covers the real need — skill-weighted auto-distribution judged unnecessary, don't build it. Printable **per-bed sheet** (two-sided, laminate-and-leave-by-the-bed) lands here as a sibling of per-person sheets; print CSS is cheap when its time comes.
- [ ] Multi-site coordination and aggregate sufficiency rollups (bed → site → town)
- [ ] Time scrubbing: view the plan as of any date, forward and back
- [ ] Preservation planner deepening: canning/curing/smoking timing tied to predicted harvest volumes
- [ ] Ornamental & pollinator beds (aesthetics + beneficial insect habitat)
- [ ] Climate drift: periodic re-derivation of climate normals (the ERA5 rolling-window design already supports this)
- [ ] Local AI integration for Q&A/planning over the homestead data
- [ ] Community crop packs; CONTRIBUTING.md with AI-use policy
- [ ] Public demo site; revisit hosted offering (donations first — hosting means real data-custody obligations)

## Inbox (capture-only, sort later)

- **Revisit later, not a defect:** conflict overrides are per-cell, so one conflict is accepted twice — accepting the tomato leaves the adjacent potato still marked. Judged fine as shipped (it is literally per-placement, and predictable); the alternative is accepting the conflicting *pair*, which gets murkier with three-way conflicts. Re-read this after some real planning use and see whether the opinion has changed.
- **Two dependency advisories parked behind the advisory-only audit job (2026-07-25).** Neither is reachable in this app and neither has a patched release we can take today, which is why CI no longer fails on them — but "not reachable" is a claim with a shelf life, so re-check when either upstream moves. (1) `react-router` 7.12.0–8.2.x CSRF, and it is real code, just only in the *unstable RSC APIs* — we are a HashRouter SPA with no RSC and no server actions. Fixed in `react-router` **8.3.0**, which we cannot simply bump to: `react-router-dom` stops at 7.18.1 because v8 folded it back into the `react-router` package, so this is a major migration to schedule deliberately. (2) `vite-plugin-pwa >= 1.3.0` → `workbox-build >= 7.4.1` and its build-time chain (ejs, jake, minimatch, brace-expansion, rollup-plugin-off-main-thread) — all devDependencies that never reach `dist`, and 7.4.1 is the newest published version, so there is nothing to upgrade to yet. Both "fixes" npm offers are downgrades. Revisit the react-router 8 migration on its own merits rather than as a security scramble.
- Auto-fill caps species variety at a fixed 6 (4 for `calories`/`yield`) however large the bed: `Math.min(6, Math.ceil(totalCells / 3))` in `recommendBedPlanting`. The low end does scale — a 6-cell bed gets 2 species — but the top end never moves, so a 96-cell bed and a 400-cell bed both get 6. Not obviously a bug: fewer types is the deliberate point of `calories`/`yield`, and monoculture blocks are legitimate at field scale. Wants sorting alongside the Level 3 rescoring and the fields abstraction rather than a blind raise.
- *(sorted from the 2026-07-25 idea batch — parked deliberately, each with its unlock condition)*
- **Sensor integration** (pH, moisture, fertility probes): no single open standard exists — the closest ecosystem is MQTT/Home Assistant. If it ever happens, the right shape is *accepting imported readings*, never device drivers. Fails the layperson test today; Level 4+ at best.
- **Soil pH gating recommendations:** coherent with the environment-check pattern (optional bed field, ignorable by default, `limitedBy`-style reason), but needs authored pH ranges per plant *and* users who know their pH. Wait for a large verified corpus. Public soil data (SoilGrids, CC-BY) could someday prefill it from location — depends on this existing first.
- **Mid-season diagnostics** ("yellowing leaves might mean…", pruning tips for poor producers): an authored plant-care encyclopedia by another name — large content cost, drifts the app toward reference book. The Level 4 local-AI Q&A line is the cheaper route to the same value; re-evaluate against it, not on its own.
- **Culling recommendations:** not a feature — an *output* of things already planned. End of computed window + rotation memory + backwards planning ≈ "this window closed, the next crop wants in." Don't build a separate culling engine.
- **Forever bed** (continuously replanted day-by-day, never reset between seasons): revisit once succession presets and backwards planning both exist — it may fall out of them nearly free. Standalone, it's a combinatorial money pit.
- **Rice paddy:** a new (flooded) environment type plus water infrastructure; only sensible after Level 4 water planning. Kept so it isn't lost — it's fun.
- **Wood pile / firewood ops** and **fiber/fabric plants** (flax, rope crops): non-food self-sufficiency. Real homestead life, but behind the food thesis — belongs to a hypothetical future "homestead ops" wing, not to any current level. Fiber is cheap if it's ever just more plants plus a use tag.
- **Vertical growing** (herb tubes, towers): a new container geometry the environment-type system could absorb someday. No pull for it yet.
- **Soil mixture / amendment recommendations from prior plantings:** the adjacent real feature is Level 3 rotation memory (season archives already hold the history). Amendment advice needs authored per-plant data; consider as a rotation-memory extension, not standalone.
- **OpenPlantbook import:** its data is sensor-threshold-oriented (min/max moisture/light for the Home Assistant crowd) and community-grade; ClimateNeeds semantics are richer than what it offers, and its licensing is unverified against the no-NC/SA rule. Skip unless the sensor idea revives.
- **localStorage → IndexedDB migration:** localStorage caps at ~5MB and rewrites the whole state as one JSON string per save; IndexedDB is the browser's real local database (async, effectively unbounded for our sizes) and is *exactly as offline* — same device, same browser, no server involved. Contained change: Zustand `persist` takes a pluggable storage backend; first launch copies `gardener-storage` over, users notice nothing. When it lands, also call `navigator.storage.persist()` so the browser won't evict data under disk pressure. Unlock condition: years of season archives + a 300-plant corpus pressing the ceiling — not before.
- **Native-only / native-wildlife auto-fill filters:** unlike low-water (data already exists), native-ness is a whole new authored data axis — range maps per plant, fuzzy by region. Park until there's a data story; don't let it ride the cheap-filter wave.
